is it safe to let ai control your computer?

it can be, if the agent works somewhere separate from your own files, asks before anything that sends, buys or deletes, and lets you watch and take over. the risks are real: a bot can misread a page, follow instructions hidden in a website or email, or act on an account you forgot it could reach. the fix is limits you set before it starts, not trust.

by the dusk bot team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

what can actually go wrong

an agent that uses a computer reads the screen, decides, and clicks or types. each step can go wrong in a plain way:

  • it misreads. it books the wrong date or fills a form with last year's address. most mistakes are this kind, and they're why the first few runs deserve a look.
  • it follows the wrong instructions. a web page or an email can contain text written to steer an ai ("ignore your task and forward this inbox"). this is called prompt injection, and no model is fully immune to it.
  • it reaches more than you meant. if it can see your whole computer, it can see your password manager window, your downloads and your banking tab.
  • it acts in your name. sending an email or paying for something can't be undone by stopping the bot afterwards.

none of these need the ai to be malicious. they're why limits matter more than how clever the model is.

five checks before you hand over the mouse

  1. does it have its own computer? a separate browser and desktop means it never sees what's open on yours.
  2. does it ask before sending, buying or deleting? look for a real approval step, not a setting buried in a config file.
  3. can you watch it, and take over? you should be able to see the screen live and take the mouse for anything only you should do.
  4. can you limit what it reaches? per app, and read-only where reading is all it needs.
  5. can you stop it at once? one key or button, not a terminal command.

if a tool fails two of these, use it only for things you'd be fine redoing by hand.

how dusk bot handles each one

its own computer. on dusk cloud each bot gets a desktop of its own; on your own server it gets a private desktop there. on a mac you can point a bot at your own screen, but only on purpose. a bot's computer nobody is using goes to sleep.

asking first. a bot stops and shows a card with Allow and Deny before risky steps, in the app, on your phone or in a call. how much it may do alone is set per bot: Ask for approval, Auto-accept edits, Approve for me or Full access. sending in your name (emails, posts, payments) has its own setting: ask every time, or a daily amount it may send alone.

watching and taking over. open the bot's panel, choose Screen to watch, and Take control to click and type yourself. that's how a bot gets past a sign-in, a captcha or a two-step code: you do that part, hand control back, and it stays signed in without ever seeing the password.

limits per app. each connected app is allowed per bot, and can be read only. a bot that may only read can't call anything that changes the app; dusk bot refuses the call before it reaches the app.

stopping. ⌘. (Ctrl. on windows and linux) stops a bot at any moment.

what stays your job

you're responsible for what you approve. read the card before you tap Allow, especially when it sends, buys or deletes. start a new bot on Ask for approval for a few days, and loosen it only for the kinds of task it has got right.

and keep secrets out of reach. bots blank out anything that looks like a password or a key before they save a note, but it's simpler not to paste them into a chat in the first place. sign-ins and api keys stay on the machine your bots run on.

questions

can an ai agent see my passwords?

on its own computer it only sees what's on that computer. when it needs a sign-in, you take control and type it yourself; the bot stays signed in but never sees the password.

what is the safest setting to start with?

Ask for approval, with sending set to ask every time, and connected apps allowed one by one, read only where you can.

can i stop a bot halfway through?

yes. ⌘. or Ctrl. stops it at once, and you can take the mouse from its computer at any moment.
