did openai buy openclaw?

no. the documented story is that peter steinberger, openclaw's creator, joined openai, while the project moved to an independent foundation. that is different from openai buying openclaw. clawd, clawdbot and moltbot are names you may meet in the project's history. as of 12 november 2026, the foundation says the project remains open and independent.1

by the dusk bot team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

why the names are confusing

the naming history starts with clawd in november 2025. after anthropic's legal team asked for a rename, the project became moltbot, then openclaw, announced on 29 january 2026. the spelling clawdbot also survives in a repository docker advisory.6

so an old tutorial title can use a name you no longer see on the homepage. that does not by itself make the tutorial fake. it does mean you should compare its date, install instructions and settings with current documentation. do not assume an old command remains the right way to install the present app just because the mascot looks familiar.

the dated timeline

  • november 2025: the naming history begins with clawd. moltbot follows the rename request; openclaw's post doesn't say exactly when.2
  • 29 january 2026: openclaw is announced under its present name.2
  • 14 february 2026: peter steinberger announces that he is joining openai and says openclaw will move to a foundation and stay independent.3
  • 8 july 2026: the openclaw foundation is announced as a us 501(c)(3), with full-time staff. its announcement says openai has committed to keeping the project open and independent.1

keep those events separate when reading a headline. a creator taking a new job, a product changing its name and a foundation being established answer different questions.

what joining openai does and does not establish

the february announcement is about the creator's move and the project's future home. the july announcement describes the foundation. neither supports telling readers that openai bought the app or that it became an openai product.13

there is still a reason the two names appear together in search results: the creator joined openai and the foundation describes a commitment from openai. that's a real link between them, but it doesn't mean openai owns it, supports it or vouches for its safety.

what openclaw is today

openclaw describes itself as an open-source personal ai assistant you run on your computer or server and use from chat apps. its repository uses the mit license. both were true on 12 november 2026.45

if you are deciding whether to run it, the historical name is less useful than the current setup and maintenance requirements. the openclaw alternative guide helps compare the shape of the work. the openclaw safety guide separates published advisories, malicious skills and the security work reported afterwards.

how to read the next announcement

check who is speaking, the date, and the exact thing announced. a maintainer's post is evidence about their own plans. a foundation announcement is evidence about the organization it describes. a software repository is where you check the current license and code history.

when someone shares an acquisition claim, ask for the acquisition announcement rather than another retelling of the founder joining a company. when someone shares a clawd or moltbot setup guide, ask which release it covers. we can't vouch for an old download, a hosting service or a lookalike name.

questions

what is the difference between clawdbot and moltbot?

they appear in the history of the project now called openclaw. the naming account says clawd, then moltbot, then openclaw; clawdbot also appears in an older repository advisory.26

who founded openclaw?

peter steinberger is identified as its creator. he announced his move to openai on 14 february 2026.3

is openclaw still independent?

the foundation announcement says it is open and independent, and says openai committed to keeping it that way. that was still the case on 12 november 2026.1
