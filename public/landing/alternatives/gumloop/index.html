gumloop alternatives when you want to give a job in a message

gumloop already offers agents, browser work and chat channels, so chat alone is not a reason to switch. choose it if you want a hosted platform for building agents and workflows. dusk bot fits a different starting point: download an app, give a few bots their jobs in plain words, and choose where they live. compare the charging model and the work you need to maintain before deciding.

by the dusk bot team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

the short version

pick gumloop if

  • you want a no-code agent and workflow builder with 400+ connectors, rather than starting from a desktop bot
  • you want hosted browser work with saved logins and a plan offering 25 concurrent agent chats

pick dusk bot if

  • you want a named bot whose job, chats and memory stay together in an app
  • you want to begin on your own computer and pay your ai provider separately
  • you would rather describe a small recurring job than maintain a built workflow

what changes if you switch

these are different ways to organise work, not a ranking of output quality. prices below are as of 12 november 2026; check the fee and credit terms as well as the headline amount.

do not switch just to get chat or a browser

gumloop describes agents that use a real browser, saved logins and your tools. its homepage also lists messages in chat, slack, teams, imessage and email.1 dusk bot is not the only conversational option here.

the useful question is how you want to give work its shape. if you like building a repeatable process, keep the builder on your shortlist. if you want to give one bot a standing job and correct it in conversation, try that style before rebuilding anything. n8n alternatives covers another workflow-first choice; zapier agents alternatives looks at app-driven work.

read the credit terms before the monthly price

gumloop pro starts at $37 a month as of 12 november 2026. gumloop's pricing page advertises 20,000 credits, split into 7,400 plus 12,600 bonus credits, and an 8% orchestration fee. unused credits do not roll over. with your own api keys, token credits drop to zero but the orchestration fee rises to 16%.2

that is not directly comparable to dusk cloud hosting. dusk bills for the hosted computer, while your ai provider bills for thinking. personal desktop use is free; work use needs a work license.5612 list both bills when comparing your own likely cost. we have no measured cost for an identical job. check your ai provider's current terms for the way you plan to use its sign-in.

try a file-based handoff first

a small first message could be: "every monday, list the five newest files in this folder and what each is about. leave the files unchanged." give the bot the folder to use and check the first list against its files.

dusk lets you attach documents and keeps a bot's files in its own folder or a project folder you give it.8 once the handoff is useful, consider a recurring version using scheduled ai tasks. a local dusk routine cannot run while the app is closed or the computer sleeps; use an awake machine or dusk cloud for jobs that must happen on time.9

how we checked

we make dusk bot. this page uses gumloop's homepage and pricing page as they read on 12 november 2026, alongside our docs. we did not run identical tasks in both products or measure speed, accuracy or cost per completed job. the comparison describes the offered setup and billing, so a plan feature here is not a promise that either bot will finish your particular task.

questions

is gumloop free or paid?

as of 12 november 2026, gumloop's pricing page shows a 14-day trial and pro from $37/month, with credits and an orchestration fee. it does not show an ongoing free tier.2

gumloop vs n8n: which should i compare first?

compare their building and billing models. gumloop lists credits and orchestration fees; n8n charges by workflow execution. neither price alone tells you the cost of your process. see the n8n comparison for n8n's prices.210

do i need a workflow builder for a weekly report?

start with what you need to control. if you mainly want a bot to read files you give it and explain differences, try a narrow chat instruction first. if a fixed sequence of steps matters, compare a workflow builder. check the first outputs yourself in either case.
