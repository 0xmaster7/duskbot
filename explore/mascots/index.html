3. palettes

three bright, vivid sets of twelve for a white page. lightness is capped per colour so pure white eyes always keep at least 2:1 (sun, lime and mint sit at the deeper end of their hue for that reason). the eye contrast is listed under each swatch. same twelve bots in every row; the dark row underneath is only a secondary check.

4. shape family

eighteen soft shapes that all obey one rule set. the eye and palette pickers in the bar above change the whole family at once.

the rule set

  1. one flat fill. no outline, no gradient, no texture, no shading. the body is a single bright colour from the palette.
  2. eyes only, pure white. two white eyes and nothing else: no pupils, glints, mouth, nose, blush or brows. all emotion is the eye shape, gaze, blink and body motion.
  3. same eyes everywhere. one eye design across the whole family, the same size on every shape (about 19 units tall in the 100 unit box), set on the optical centre of the main mass, about 40 to 45 percent down it. ears, tufts and leaves never count as mass.
  4. white must read. a body colour only joins the palette if white eyes keep at least 2:1 on it.
  5. soft everything. smallest corner radius 6 units, ears and tips included. no sharp points.
  6. one silhouette idea. the main mass is a plain shape (circle, squircle, capsule, drop). a bot may add at most one kind of extra: two ears, a tuft, a leaf, scallops.
  7. stands on the floor. every body's lowest point sits on the baseline at 88, width 56 to 80 units, so a row of bots lines up.
  8. small sizes. under 16 px use the pixel version.

5. icons

the twenty two icons the app leans on most (counted from the app's imports), in five candidates. hover a row to play its motion, or press play all. the motion layer is about 1.5 kb of plain css, so it works on the site without react and on any of these sets.

recommendation

gravity ui icons + our tiny css motion layer. it is the look you pointed at: crisp 16 px grid, a mix of outline and solid weight that stays sharp at the 14 to 18 px sizes the app uses, and it reads modern next to the round bots. mit licence, about 570 bytes per svg (320 gzipped), no runtime: inline svg or a sprite on the site, the same files in the app. the motion is ours (transform only, short, hover and press only, off under reduced motion), so nothing new to install anywhere.

gaps: gravity has 809 icons against lucide's 1,700. it has no laptop, audio wave, building, phone handset or spinner, so keep lucide for those few or draw them in the same grid.

runner up: lucide + lucide-animated (pqoqubbw/icons, mit). it is what the app already uses and the app already ships motion, so the animated versions drop in with zero new packages, and they have the nicest motion (paths draw in). but they are react components; on the site we would port the motions to css, as the last column does.

not picked: phosphor (mit, friendly, six weights; a close third). tabler (mit, solid but the most generic). animate-ui icons (mit plus commons clause, react plus motion only). itshover (apache 2.0, react plus motion). lordicon (lottie runtime, free tier needs attribution).

every shape and pixel here is our own drawing. bloub and the xai bot were used only as a bar for how alive a blob can feel; no shapes, paths, proportions, states or timing came from them. licences for the vendored icons are in licenses/.
